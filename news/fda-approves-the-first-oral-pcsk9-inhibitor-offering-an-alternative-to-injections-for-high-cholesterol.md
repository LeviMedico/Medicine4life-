* #### The FDA has approved Lipfendra (enlicitide), the first oral PCSK9 inhibitor for adults with high LDL ("bad") cholesterol, including familial hypercholesterolemia. Until now, every drug in this class — including well-known options like Repatha and Praluent — required regular injections, since PCSK9 inhibitors are typically large biologic molecules that can't survive digestion if swallowed. Lipfendra gets around that using a novel "macrocyclic peptide" structure, engineered specifically to remain stable and effective as a once-daily pill.

  The approval is backed by two Phase 3 trials. In the CORALreef Lipids trial, Lipfendra reduced LDL cholesterol by 56% compared to placebo at 24 weeks; in patients with familial hypercholesterolemia, the reduction was 59%. Both results are in the same range as existing injectable PCSK9 inhibitors, meaning the pill isn't just more convenient — it appears to work about as well. Side effects were largely similar to placebo, with diarrhea and dizziness reported somewhat more often in the familial hypercholesterolemia trial.

  Why this matters beyond the convenience factor: an estimated 6 million Americans have LDL cholesterol high enough to qualify for a PCSK9 inhibitor even while already on a statin, yet only about 1% currently take one. Cost and insurance approval play a role, but so does the injection itself — the needle, the pharmacy logistics, and refrigeration requirements have long been a real barrier. Removing that barrier could meaningfully expand who actually uses this drug class, though a large outcomes trial (over 14,000 participants) is still underway to confirm whether Lipfendra's cholesterol-lowering effect translates into fewer actual heart attacks and strokes over time — the same standard already established for its injectable predecessors.
