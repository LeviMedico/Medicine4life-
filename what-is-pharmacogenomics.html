What is pharmacogenomics?

Medicine4life · July 16, 2026

Pharmacogenomics is the study of how your genes affect the way your body responds to drugs. Specifically, it looks at how quickly you break a drug down, and whether a standard dose is likely to work well, do nothing, or cause side effects — for you personally.

The short version: two people can take the exact same pill at the exact same dose and have completely different experiences, because their bodies process it differently at a genetic level. Certain genes control enzymes in the liver that metabolize drugs — some people have variants that make those enzymes fast, some slow, and some right in the middle.

This isn't just theoretical. It already changes real prescribing decisions for a specific list of drugs — including some blood thinners, certain chemotherapy drugs, and several antidepressants — where testing can flag ahead of time if a standard dose is likely to be wrong for that patient.

It's not yet a routine part of every prescription, mostly because the strong evidence only covers a specific set of drug-gene pairs, not medications in general. But it's a fast-growing area, and one more sign that "one dose fits all" is slowly becoming outdated.
