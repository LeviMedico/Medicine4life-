If you rank FMGE subjects by how many questions they typically carry, Community Medicine (PSM) usually sits at or near the top — yet it's consistently the subject students revise last, if at all. That mismatch between weightage and preparation time is exactly where marks get left on the table.

## Why PSM feels harder to prepare than it should be

Unlike Medicine or Surgery, PSM doesn't have a small number of "big disease" topics you can drill deeply. It's wide and list-heavy — immunization schedules, epidemiological study designs, health programs, biostatistics, demography, and nutrition all show up as scattered one-off questions rather than one dominant theme. That breadth is exactly why cramming it in the last few days rarely works; you need repeated, spaced exposure to the lists themselves.

## Where the actual FMGE questions concentrate

Based on repeatedly tested areas across past exams, a few sections consistently deliver disproportionate returns:

- **National Health Programs** — especially eligibility criteria, target groups, and recent program name changes (programs get renamed and restructured periodically, and FMGE tends to test the current name and current targets)
- **Immunization schedule** — exact ages, number of doses, and route of administration for each vaccine in the National Immunization Schedule
- **Epidemiological study designs** — knowing how to instantly identify a case-control vs. cohort vs. cross-sectional study from a one-line scenario, since this shows up almost every attempt
- **Biostatistics basics** — sensitivity/specificity, screening test evaluation, and measures of central tendency, tested more for application than pure definition
- **Nutrition** — deficiency disorders and their classic clinical signs, plus recommended dietary allowances for specific groups

<p class="pull">PSM rewards breadth of exposure over depth in any one topic — five minutes on ten different lists beats one hour on a single list.</p>

## A practical way to revise it

Rather than reading PSM start to finish once, cycle through the high-yield lists above multiple times in short sessions — immunization schedule today, study designs tomorrow, back to immunization three days later. The spaced repetition matters more here than in almost any other FMGE subject, because the content is inherently list-based rather than conceptual.

## The bottom line

PSM isn't the most "interesting" subject to revise, but pound for pound, it's one of the highest-yield uses of your remaining prep time — precisely because so many other students deprioritize it until the last week, if they get to it at all.
