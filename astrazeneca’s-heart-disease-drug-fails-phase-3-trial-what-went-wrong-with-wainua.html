"AstraZeneca’s Heart Disease Drug Fails Phase 3 Trial: What Went Wrong With Wainua?"

Sushil Dethaliya · July 14, 2026

Category: Medical Research | Cardiology | Drug Development

Introduction

A promising heart disease treatment developed by AstraZeneca and Ionis Pharmaceuticals has suffered a major setback after failing to achieve its main goal in a late-stage clinical trial.

The drug, Wainua (eplontersen), was being investigated as a potential treatment for transthyretin-mediated amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) — a rare but serious condition in which abnormal protein deposits accumulate in the heart and gradually impair its function.

The Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial failed to show a statistically significant reduction in cardiovascular deaths and recurring cardiovascular events compared with placebo, despite the drug showing a favourable safety profile.

What Is ATTR-CM?

Transthyretin-mediated amyloid cardiomyopathy is a form of cardiac amyloidosis , a disease caused by the buildup of misfolded transthyretin (TTR) proteins in heart tissue.

Normally, transthyretin is a transport protein produced mainly by the liver. However, in ATTR-CM, unstable TTR proteins break apart and form amyloid deposits.

These deposits make the heart muscle stiff, reducing its ability to relax and pump blood efficiently.

Common symptoms include:

  • Shortness of breath
  • Fatigue
  • Swelling of legs
  • Irregular heartbeat
  • Heart failure symptoms

Because symptoms often resemble common heart diseases, ATTR-CM can remain undiagnosed for years.

How Was Wainua Supposed to Work?

Wainua (eplontersen) belongs to a new generation of RNA-targeted medicines called antisense oligonucleotides.

Instead of directly removing amyloid deposits, the drug works earlier in the disease pathway.

Mechanism of action:

  1. Wainua binds to messenger RNA responsible for producing transthyretin.

  2. It reduces production of both normal and abnormal TTR proteins in the liver.

  3. Lower TTR levels may reduce the formation of new amyloid deposits.

  4. Over time, this could potentially slow heart damage.

This approach represented a major advancement because it targeted the root cause of amyloid formation rather than only managing symptoms.

What Happened in the Phase 3 Trial?

The CARDIO-TTRansform Phase 3 trial evaluated Wainua in patients with ATTR-CM.

The study compared patients receiving eplontersen with those receiving placebo while receiving modern standard medical care.

The main goal was a composite measure:

  • Cardiovascular-related death
  • Recurrent cardiovascular events

After approximately 140 weeks of follow-up, the trial did not meet its primary endpoint. Researchers found that adding Wainua to standard treatment did not provide a statistically significant improvement compared with placebo.

Why Did the Drug Fail?

The failure does not necessarily mean the drug was ineffective. Several scientific factors may explain the unexpected result.

  1. Modern background treatments may have reduced the detectable benefit

Many participants were already receiving existing ATTR therapies, including TTR stabilisers.

When patients are already benefiting from effective treatments, proving that an additional drug provides extra improvement becomes much more difficult.

The trial investigators noted that many patients were receiving stabiliser therapy, which may have influenced the ability to detect additional benefit from Wainua.

2. Disease biology may be more complex than expected

Reducing TTR production is scientifically logical, but ATTR-CM progression depends on multiple factors:

  • Existing amyloid deposits
  • Duration of disease
  • Heart damage before treatment
  • Patient genetics
  • Age-related changes

A drug can successfully reduce a disease-causing protein but still fail to produce measurable improvements in survival or major cardiovascular outcomes within a clinical trial period.

3. The trial endpoint was extremely challenging

Cardiovascular mortality is one of the hardest endpoints to improve in clinical research.

A treatment may show biological activity — such as reducing TTR levels — but demonstrating fewer deaths requires:

  • Large patient populations
  • Long follow-up periods
  • Strong treatment effects

Many promising cardiovascular drugs fail because the clinical benefit is smaller than expected.

Did Wainua Show Any Positive Signals?

Although the overall trial failed, researchers observed a potential benefit in a pre-specified subgroup of patients receiving Wainua alone compared with placebo.

This finding was described as nominally significant , but further analysis is needed before drawing conclusions.

The companies plan additional evaluation of the complete dataset.

Impact on AstraZeneca and the Pharmaceutical Industry

The failure affected investor confidence because Wainua was expected to become an important part of AstraZeneca’s cardiovascular and metabolic disease pipeline.

Following the announcement, AstraZeneca and Ionis shares dropped significantly as markets reacted to the trial setback.

However, clinical failures are a normal part of drug development. Even large pharmaceutical companies experience setbacks because only a fraction of experimental medicines successfully reach patients.

What Does This Mean for ATTR-CM Patients?

The failure of Wainua does not mean progress in ATTR-CM treatment has stopped.

Other therapeutic approaches continue to develop, including:

  • TTR stabilisers
  • RNA interference therapies
  • Gene-editing approaches
  • Combination treatments

The field remains one of the fastest-growing areas in cardiovascular medicine.

The Future of Precision Medicine in Heart Disease

Wainua’s setback highlights an important lesson in modern medicine:

Understanding the molecular cause of a disease does not always guarantee immediate clinical success.

Precision medicine requires not only targeting the correct biological pathway but also selecting the right patients, timing treatment correctly, and measuring outcomes that truly reflect patient benefit.

As researchers continue studying ATTR-CM, future therapies may combine multiple approaches to prevent protein misfolding, remove existing deposits, and protect heart function.

Conclusion

AstraZeneca and Ionis’ Wainua represented one of the most advanced attempts to treat ATTR-CM through RNA-targeted therapy. Although the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform trial failed to achieve its main goal, the results provide valuable information for future drug development.

The setback demonstrates the complexity of treating heart diseases at the molecular level — and why every clinical trial, successful or unsuccessful, contributes to the future of medicine.
