## *In a significant advancement for diabetes management, Novo Nordisk has officially launched Awiqli (insulin icodec) in India, introducing the country's first once-weekly basal insulin therapy. The launch offers a new treatment option for adults with diabetes who require long-acting insulin and has the potential to reduce the burden of daily injection*s.


India is home to one of the world's largest populations of people living with diabetes, with more than 100 million affected individuals. For many patients, the need for daily insulin injections can be challenging, often leading to poor adherence and inadequate blood glucose control. Awiqli aims to address this challenge by requiring only one injection every week instead of one every day.


Clinical studies have demonstrated that insulin icodec provides blood sugar control comparable to daily basal insulin in appropriately selected patients. By reducing the number of injections from 365 to just 52 each year, the therapy may improve convenience, treatment adherence, and overall quality of life.


Novo Nordisk has introduced Awiqli in India in two prefilled pen presentations. The company has stated that the pricing has been designed to remain competitive with existing basal insulin therapies, making the treatment more accessible for eligible patients.


Despite its convenience, healthcare experts emphasize that Awiqli is not a replacement for all insulin therapies. It is a basal (long-acting) insulin intended to maintain background insulin levels and does not replace rapid-acting insulin required before meals in many patients. Any transition from daily insulin to once-weekly insulin should be made only under the supervision of a qualified healthcare professional.


The launch places India among the first countries globally to offer this innovative therapy, reflecting the country's growing access to advanced diabetes treatments.

As diabetes continues to impose a major public health burden, innovations such as once-weekly insulin could play an important role in improving long-term disease management and patient adherence. Continued patient education, careful clinical monitoring, and individualized treatment decisions will remain essential to achieving the best outcomes.
