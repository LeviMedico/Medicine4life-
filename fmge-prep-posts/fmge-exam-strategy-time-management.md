FMGE tests a huge breadth of medical knowledge, but a meaningful part of the challenge isn't knowing the material — it's answering 300 questions inside a fixed time window without losing easy marks to poor pacing. Content prep gets most of the attention; exam-day strategy gets comparatively little, even though it can be the difference between a narrow pass and a narrow fail.

## Know the actual pass mark, and plan around it

FMGE requires getting roughly half the total marks correct to pass — this isn't an exam where you need to know everything, it's an exam where you need to reliably capture the easy and moderate questions without giving away marks through rushed mistakes or panic on hard questions. That reframes the goal: the priority is protecting your accuracy on the questions you *do* know, not stressing over subjects you feel weak in.

## A practical time-management approach

- **First pass — the questions you know instantly.** Move through the paper answering only what you're immediately confident about, and mark everything else to revisit. Don't get stuck on a single hard question during this pass.
- **Second pass — the "maybe" questions.** These are the ones where you can eliminate one or two options but aren't fully sure. This is where careful reasoning earns real marks.
- **Last pass — genuine guesses.** Since FMGE typically doesn't penalize wrong answers beyond not scoring, an educated guess is still better than leaving a question blank, once you're confident there's no negative marking in the specific exam cycle you're sitting — always confirm the current marking scheme before assuming this.

<p class="pull">Losing five minutes to one hard question you were never going to get right is five minutes stolen from three easy questions you would have gotten right.</p>

## Common self-inflicted mistakes worth avoiding

- **Overthinking single-best-answer questions** — FMGE questions are usually written to have one clearly best answer; second-guessing a confident first read is more often wrong than right
- **Skipping the clinical vignette details** — a scenario question's answer often hinges on one specific detail (age, duration, a single lab value) that's easy to skim past under time pressure
- **Uneven time allocation across subjects** — spending disproportionate time on a personally "favorite" subject at the expense of others, even when marks per question are identical everywhere

## The week before the exam

This is a better time for structured revision of high-yield lists (immunization schedules, drug-of-choice tables, classic clinical associations) than for attempting new topics from scratch. Trying to learn unfamiliar material in the final days tends to crowd out confident recall of things you already know well — which is a worse trade than it sounds.

## The bottom line

FMGE preparation is usually treated as a pure content-memorization problem, but pacing, question-selection strategy, and protecting your accuracy on known material are just as much a part of the actual score as how much you've studied. Both matter — and the second one is free to improve with no additional content review required.
