## A major milestone for precision oncology



The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted early approval to Jideytro, GSK's newest targeted cancer medicine for adults with ROS1-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously received treatment. The approval arrived nearly two months before the expected decision date, highlighting the FDA's confidence in the available clinical evidence.



The approval also marks GSK's official entry into the highly competitive market for targeted lung cancer therapies following its $10.6 billion acquisition of Nuvalent, the biotechnology company that developed the drug.



### What is ROS1-positive lung cancer?



ROS1-positive NSCLC is a rare subtype of lung cancer caused by alterations in the ROS1 gene, which drives uncontrolled tumor growth. It accounts for roughly 1–2% of non-small cell lung cancers.



Patients with ROS1-positive tumors often respond well to targeted therapies, which specifically block the abnormal protein while generally causing fewer side effects than traditional chemotherapy.



### How effective is Jideytro?



The FDA approval was supported by clinical trial data involving 117 previously treated patients.



### Key findings included:

\- 44% overall response rate, meaning nearly half of patients experienced significant tumor shrinkage.

\- Many responses were durable, with a substantial proportion of patients maintaining their response for six months or longer.

\- The treatment demonstrated meaningful activity in patients whose disease had progressed after earlier therapies.

### Why this approval matters

Targeted therapies have transformed lung cancer treatment by matching medicines to specific genetic mutations instead of treating all tumors the same way.

Jideytro expands the treatment options available for patients with ROS1-positive disease and will compete with other targeted medicines already used for this mutation, including Augtyro, Rozlytrek, and Xalkori.

### Looking ahead

GSK expects Jideytro to become available in U.S. pharmacies within weeks. The company is also continuing to expand its oncology portfolio with additional targeted cancer medicines currently under regulatory review.
